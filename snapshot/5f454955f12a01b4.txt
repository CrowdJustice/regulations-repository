        <h1>Independence and high standards ensure lawyers help migrants access justice</h1>
        <h2>2 October 2026</h2>

<p>An independent legal profession plays a vital role in ensuring everyone in Australia, including migrants, can understand and assert their rights under the law.</p>

<p>&quot;Lawyers acting in migration matters are bound by the same stringent ethical and professional obligations as every other lawyer, including the duty never to mislead a court, tribunal or government decision-maker. Where those obligations are breached, there should be consequences, and the regulatory framework in every state and territory provides for them,&quot; Law Council President Tania Wolff said.</p>

<p>&quot;If the Department holds evidence of misconduct, it should be referred to the relevant regulator without delay. Regulators can investigate and impose a range of sanctions, including fines, conditions on a practising certificate, suspension or removal from practice. Courts can also refer practitioners to regulators.</p>

<p>&quot;Great care is needed, though, not to suggest a systemic failure across the profession. Migration law is complex, the stakes for clients are profound, and independent legal advice is part of how the system works fairly.</p>

<p>&quot;A lawyer&#39;s paramount duty is to the court and the administration of justice. Lawyers must exercise independent judgement rather than act as a mouthpiece for their client, and must not engage in conduct likely to diminish public confidence in the administration of justice.</p>

<p>In migration litigation, the<em> Migration Act 1958</em> (Cth) also requires lawyers to certify that proceedings have reasonable prospects of success, and those who encourage hopeless proceedings can face personal costs orders.&quot;</p>

<p>Ms Wolff said some of the matters raised today needed to be understood in context.</p>

<p>&quot;Without knowing the facts of particular cases, it&#39;s important to understand that the Minister&#39;s personal power under section 351 of the Migration Act to make a more favourable decision in the public interest is generally only available after a visa has been refused and that refusal affirmed by the Administrative Review Tribunal. For people with genuinely compelling circumstances, that may be the only pathway available. Where a lawyer is open with the Department and the Tribunal about why an application is being made, following the process Parliament designed is not improper.</p>

<p>&quot;The Law Council stands ready to discuss any genuine concerns with the Government. We urge consultation with the profession and underline the need to preserve its essential independence.&quot;</p>

<p>Contact: Kristen Connell | 0400 054 227 | kristen.connell@lawcouncil.au</p>
