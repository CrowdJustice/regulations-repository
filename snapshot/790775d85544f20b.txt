      <div class="govspeak"><h2 id="introduction">Introduction </h2>

<div class="call-to-action">
  <p>The UK government’s sanctions lists changed to a single list on Wednesday 28 January 2026. UK sanctions designations are now only detailed in the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uk-sanctions-list">UK Sanctions List</a> (<abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr>), published by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office.</p>

  <p>The Consolidated List of Asset Freeze Targets, which was published for HM Treasury by the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (<abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr>) is no longer being updated. It is <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/financial-sanctions-consolidated-list-of-targets">available for reference purposes on its old page</a>.</p>
</div>

<p>This guidance is to help you understand manage the change, in particular for businesses and industry that regularly use the UK Sanctions List and the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List for sanctions compliance. Your systems now need to use data from the UK Sanctions List.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-the-uk-sanctions-list">What is the UK Sanctions List</h2>

<p>The UK Sanctions List has been the primary list of sanctions designations made under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 (SAMLA) since launching in 2020. It provides a comprehensive list of those persons placed under sanctions through regulations made under SAMLA. The sanctions restrictions can include financial, immigration, trade or transport sanctions.  This differs from the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List which provided information on those subject to UK financial sanctions only.  </p>

<h2 id="a-single-list-for-uk-sanctions-designations">A single list for UK sanctions designations</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sanctions-implementation-and-enforcement-cross-government-review-may-2025/cross-government-review-of-sanctions-implementation-and-enforcement">Cross-government review of sanctions implementation and enforcement</a> announced in May 2025 that the UK will be moving to a single list for all UK sanctions designations. This is in response to industry feedback that a single list will remove duplication of effort and simplify checks of who is subject to UK sanctions. </p>

<p>From 9am (UK time) on Wednesday 28 January 2026, the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uk-sanctions-list">UK Sanctions List</a>  (<abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr>) became the only sanctions list which details sanctions designations published by the UK government. The <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List and its search tool are no longer being updated . </p>

<h2 id="what-do-i-need-to-do">What do I need to do</h2>

<p>You will need to ensure that any systems that use the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List for sanctions screening purposes are instead using the data from the UK Sanctions List. Any systems that use ‘<abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Group ID’ as an identifier now need to use the UK Sanctions List’s ‘Unique ID’ for new <abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr>. </p>

<p>All UK Sanctions List formats will retain historic <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Group ID identifiers for <abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr> designated prior to 28 January 2026. These historic Group IDs will remain valid for use, for example in licence applications, frozen asset or suspected breach reporting, though <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> Unique IDs can also be used.</p>

<p> If you use a specialist screener or other third-party providers, you should speak to your supplier to understand the impact, if any, on data you receive.</p>

<h2 id="how-to-manage-the-change">How to manage the change </h2>

<p>Make sure you understand the impact of the closure of the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List to you and your organisation. Alongside this guidance, we will continue to engage with businesses and industry.</p>

<p>The <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uk-sanctions-list">UK Sanctions List</a> should now be used as your primary source of UK sanctions designations data.</p>

<p>Which format of the UK Sanctions List you use is a decision for you and your organisation.</p>

<h2 id="uk-sanctions-list-formats">UK Sanctions List formats </h2>

<p>There will be no change to formatting of existing <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> file types. New file types were published to match those offered by the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List.</p>

<p>Alongside the original 4 <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> file types:</p>

<ul>
  <li>ODT</li>
  <li>ODS</li>
  <li>XML</li>
  <li>HTML</li>
</ul>

<p>an additional 3 formats are now published:</p>

<ul>
  <li>TXT</li>
  <li>CSV</li>
  <li>PDF</li>
</ul>

<p>The URLs for each of these 7 <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> formats on GOV.UK are now static. Having these static links means URLs for each format will stay the same no matter how many times the list is updated/refreshed. The current published format for the <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> will otherwise not be modified, retaining the same data fields in the same order.</p>

<p>Details about the <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> fields and mappings from the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List fields are available on the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/guidance/format-guide-for-the-uk-sanctions-list">Format guide for the UK Sanctions List</a>.</p>

<h3 id="the-ofsi-group-id-identifier-is-being-retired">The ‘<abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Group ID’ identifier is being retired </h3>

<p>There will be a change to how newly designated persons (<abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr>) subject to financial sanctions are identified. </p>

<p>Any external systems that use ‘<abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Group ID’ to identify <abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr> subject to financial sanctions will need to be updated to use the UK Sanctions List ‘Unique ID’ and ‘Sanction Type’ fields instead.</p>

<p>Currently all <abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr> and specified ships are given a ‘Unique ID’. Those <abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr> subject to financial sanctions are also given an ‘<abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Group ID’. </p>

<p>From 28 January 2026, newly designated <abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr> subject to financial sanctions will not be assigned an ‘<abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Group ID’ and will only have a ‘Unique ID’ as an identifier. </p>

<p>All <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> formats will retain historic <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Group ID identifiers for <abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr> designated prior to this date. These historic Group IDs will remain valid for use, for example in licence applications, frozen asset or suspected breach reporting, though <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> Unique IDs can also be used.</p>

<h3 id="sanctions-list-search-tool">Sanctions list search tool</h3>

<p>A <a rel="external" href="https://search-uk-sanctions-list.service.gov.uk/">UK Sanctions List search tool</a> has been available on GOV.UK since July 2024. Filters allow users to search for <abbr title="Designated persons">DPs</abbr> specifically subject to an asset freeze. The UK Sanctions List search tool has been upgraded and now offers:</p>

<ul>
  <li>fuzzy logic search</li>
  <li>ranked search results</li>
  <li>highlighted matches</li>
  <li>improved downloads</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="sanctions-notices">Sanctions notices </h2>

<p>Financial sanctions notices used to published when updates were made to the UK Sanctions List. These notices were published when new listings, amendments, de-listings and corrections are made for financial sanctions designations only. </p>

<p>Sanctions  notices for all types of sanctions designations will now be published following an update to the UK Sanctions List. From 28 January 2026, new sanctions notices will be located alongside the relevant sanctions guidance at <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/uk-sanctions-regimes-under-the-sanctions-act">Current UK sanctions regimes</a>. Historic notices will remain on the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/financial-sanctions-regime-specific-consolidated-lists-and-releases"><abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> financial sanctions regime pages</a>.</p>

<h3 id="russia-list-of-persons-named-in-relation-to-financial-and-investment-restrictions">Russia: list of persons named in relation to financial and investment restrictions</h3>

<p>The ‘Russia: list of persons named in relation to financial and investment restrictions’ used to be published alongside the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List. These 11 entities are still subject to specific sectoral financial and investment restrictions under <a rel="external" href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2019/855/schedule/2">Schedule 2 of the Russia regulations</a>. This list is not part of the UK Sanctions List and is available on GOV.UK at: <a rel="external" href="https://government/admin/detailed-guides/1742969">Russia: list of persons named in relation to financial and investment restrictions.</a></p>

<h2 id="checklist">Checklist</h2>

<p>Do ensure you are able to screen against the UK Sanctions List and not the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List (which is no longer being updated from 28 January 2026).</p>

<p>Do look at the new formats of the UK Sanctions List and the new static URLs and consider whether your systems are using the up-to-date formats and links.</p>

<p>Do use the upgraded <abbr title="UK Sanctions List">UKSL</abbr> search tool and not the <abbr title="Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation">OFSI</abbr> Consolidated List search tool.</p>

<p>Do ensure you are signed up to <a rel="external" href="https://public.govdelivery.com/accounts/UKORGESO/subscriber/new">get UK sanctions email alerts</a>.</p>

<h2 id="further-information">Further information </h2>

<p>For general information on sanctions or queries that relate to the UK Sanctions List: contact the Foreign, Commonwealth &amp; Development Office’s Sanctions Directorate on <a href="mailto:Fcdo.correspondence@fcdo.gov.uk">Fcdo.correspondence@fcdo.gov.uk</a></p>
