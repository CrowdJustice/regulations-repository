    <h2>Overview</h2>
    <p>The Administrative Review Council is conducting an inquiry into the administrative review of social security decisions by Services Australia and the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART). The inquiry follows a request by the former Attorney-General, the Hon Mark Dreyfus KC MP, as well as a recommendation of the Senate Committee for Legal and Constitutional Affairs.</p>

<p>There are currently 3 levels of administrative review of social security primary decisions:</p>

<ol>
	<li><strong>Internal review within Services Australia</strong>. An applicant may request an explanation of the original decision by a subject matter expert and/or seek formal review of the decision by an Authorised Review Officer.</li>
	<li><strong>First review in the ART</strong>. This is meant to be informal, accessible, trauma-informed and quick. It aims to make reviews as efficient and effective as possible, minimising the burden on applicants. The applicant is usually the only participating party.</li>
	<li><strong>Second review in the ART</strong>. The Department of Social Services will usually participate in the review as the respondent agency. There may be some form of dispute resolution process such as case conferencing, mediation or conciliation to seek to resolve the matter. If the matter is not resolved, it proceeds to a hearing.</li>
</ol>

<p>The Council's inquiry will consider whether the current structures and procedures in place for the ART's review of social security and family assistance decisions are effectively delivering the objectives of the ART under the <em>Administrative Review Tribunal Act 2024</em> (Cth) (ART Act).</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ag.gov.au/legal-system/publications/arc-social-security-inquiry-terms-reference" target="_blank">Read the Terms of Reference for the inquiry</a>.</p>

<p>Read the Issues Paper:</p>

<ul>
	<li><a href="user_uploads/arc-social-security-issues-paper.pdf" target="_blank">ARC Social Security Issues Paper [PDF 640KB]</a></li>
	<li><a href="user_uploads/arc-social-security-issues-paper.docx">ARC Social Security Issues Paper [DOCX 820KB]</a></li>
</ul>

<h2>Why the Council is consulting</h2>

<p>The Council is seeking views on how each stage of the social security review process is currently working and whether there are any changes that should be made.</p>

<p>To guide consideration, the Council has prepared an issues paper that sets out some of the key issues and debates arising in connection with the various stages of review.</p>

<p>The Council invites submissions responding to the questions in the issues paper to inform its inquiry and report to the Attorney-General. You do not need to answer every question. You are welcome to respond only to those questions that are relevant to you or your organisation.</p>

<h2>Who the Council wants to hear from</h2>

<p>The Council invites comment from any stakeholders who have experience, expertise or interest in the review of social security decisions. This includes:</p>

<ul>
	<li>social security recipients and applicants</li>
	<li>welfare rights advocates and other advocacy bodies</li>
	<li>legal practitioners and peak legal bodies, including Legal Aid Commissions, Community Legal Centres, and Bar Associations</li>
	<li>academics</li>
	<li>government agencies.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to share your views</h2>

<p>The Council invites individuals and organisations to share their views via the 'Make a submission' link below.</p>

<p>The Administrative Review Council Secretariat within the Attorney-General's Department will handle the submissions received and share these with the Council.</p>

<p>Your submission may be published unless you request that your submission be kept confidential or if the Council considers that the submission should not be made public. The Council may also refer to responses, and the names of submitters (if provided), in its inquiry report.</p>

<p>The Council reserves the right not to publish submissions that contain personal information, inappropriate or defamatory content, or that may otherwise be unlawful to publish. The Council may also redact parts of published submissions if appropriate.</p>

<p>Please <strong>do not provide</strong>:</p>

<ul>
	<li>detailed information about personal legal matters or cases</li>
	<li>personal information relating to third parties</li>
	<li>information that is the subject of legal restriction (for example, under section 204 of the <em>Social Security (Administration) Act 1999</em>).</li>
</ul>

<p>Where that information is provided, the Council may not be able to consider your submission in its review.</p>

<p>Any submission provided on a confidential basis remains subject to the <em>Freedom of Information Act 1982</em> (Cth). The Attorney-General's Department is bound by the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) in the <em>Privacy Act 1988</em> (Cth). The APPs regulate how the department collects, uses, stores and discloses personal information and how you may seek access to, or correction of, the personal information that the department holds about you. Refer to the <a href="https://www.ag.gov.au/about-us/accountability-and-reporting/privacy-policy">Attorney-General's Department's Privacy Policy</a> for more information.</p>
