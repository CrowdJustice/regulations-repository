    <h2>Feedback updated 15 Sep 2026</h2>
    <div>
        <h3>We asked</h3>
        <div><p><span><span><span><span><span>Between 16 January and 1 March 2026, we held a public consultation on the operation of the <i>Statutory Declarations Act 1959</i> (the Statutory Declarations Act). This was done aspart of a statutory review, which was required under section&#160;16 of the Statutory Declarations Act. </span></span></span></span></span></p>

<p><span><span><span><span><span>The review follows significant reforms introduced through the <i>Statutory Declarations Amendment Act 2023 </i>(the Amendment Act), which commenced on 1 January 2024. Key changes included:</span></span></span></span></span></p>

<ul>
	<li><span><span><span><span><span>the launch of a digital service that enables people to make a statutory declaration online without a witness</span></span></span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span><span><span>permanently enabling the use of electronic signatures and video-link witnessing (while still retaining the traditional paper-and-ink method).</span></span></span></span></span></li>
</ul>

<p><span><span><span><span><span>The consultation sought views on the effectiveness of the operation of the Statutory Declarations Act and whether further changes were necessary or desirable. </span></span></span></span></span></p>
</div>
        <h3>You said</h3>
        <div><p><span><span><span>We received 29 submissions to the consultation from a range of stakeholders, including: </span></span></span></p>

<ul>
	<li><span><span><span>community organisations</span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span>peak bodies</span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span>legal practitioners</span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span>private sector organisations</span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span>government agencies. </span></span></span></li>
</ul>

<p><span><span><span>Stakeholders expressed broad support for the reforms, generally confirming that the expanded digital and electronic execution options have improved efficiency and convenience for Australians making Commonwealth statutory declarations. Stakeholders acknowledged that retaining paper-based execution alongside the expanded options is important to ensure accessibility and choice. </span></span></span></p>

<p><span><span><span>Stakeholders also suggested improvements to the Commonwealth statutory declarations framework, such as:</span></span></span></p>

<ul>
	<li><span><span><span>greater education and guidance, including on the digital and electronic methods of execution for the public, to grow awareness and process integrity</span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span>additional functionality for the digital Commonwealth statutory declaration service to enhance user experience</span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span>strengthened alignment with the <i>Digital ID Act 2024</i> to streamline processes</span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span>targeted expansion of the approved witness list to improve accessibility</span></span></span></li>
	<li><span><span><span>Commonwealth recognition of statutory declarations validly executed under state and territory legislation, to encourage greater consistency across frameworks.</span></span></span></li>
</ul>
</div>
        <h3>We did</h3>
        <div><p>We used the feedback received through this consultation to inform the final report of the review, <a href="https://www.ag.gov.au/node/7318">Statutory Review of the <em>Statutory Declarations Act 1959</em> Final Report</a>. The report was tabled in Parliament on 14 September 2026 and sets out the review's findings and recommendations on the operation of the Commonwealth statutory declarations framework.</p>

<p>We thank everyone who engaged in this consultation process.</p>
</div>

    <h2>Overview</h2>
    <p>The Attorney-General&#8217;s Department is seeking views on the operation of the <i>Statutory Declarations Act 1959</i> (the Act) and the <i>Statutory Declarations Regulations 2023</i> (the Regulations) as part of a statutory review (the review).</p>

<p>Section 16 of the Act requires a review of the operation of the Act to be conducted as soon as practicable after 1 January 2026 (that is, the second anniversary of the commencement of the <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2023A00092/asmade/text"><i>Statutory Declarations Amendment Act 2023</i></a> (the Amendment Act) on 1 January 2024). Under the Act, the review must consider whether the operation of the Act is effective, and whether amendments are necessary or desirable to improve its operation.</p>

<p>The Amendment Act provided Australians with more choice and convenience when making a Commonwealth statutory declaration. The principal change was the launch of a digital service that enables people to make a declaration without a witness, using myID, the Australian Government&#8217;s digital ID provider, and myGov. The reforms also permanently enabled the use of electronic signatures and video-link witnessing (following temporary changes enacted during COVID-19), while still retaining the traditional paper-and-ink method.</p>

<h3>Discussion Paper</h3>

<ul>
	<li><a href="user_uploads/statutory_review_statutory_declarations_act_paper.pdf">Statutory Review of the Statutory Declarations Act 1959 &#8211; Discussion Paper [PDF]</a></li>
	<li><a href="user_uploads/statutory_review_statutory_declarations_act_paper.docx">Statutory Review of the Statutory Declarations Act 1959 &#8211; Discussion Paper [DOCX]</a></li>
</ul>

<h3>Terms of Reference</h3>

<p>The review will examine the following matters in relation to the Act and Regulations:</p>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">
	<li>whether the operation of the Act and Regulations is effective</li>
	<li>whether amendments to the Act or Regulations are necessary or desirable to improve their operation.</li>
</ol>

<p>In considering these matters, the review will examine the following issues:</p>

<ul>
	<li>To what extent has the expansion of execution&#160;options&#160;improved&#160;efficiency and convenience for Australians when&#160;making a Commonwealth statutory declaration?</li>
	<li>Are current safeguards operating effectively?</li>
	<li>Are any changes needed to improve the operation of the Act&#160;and&#160;Regulations more broadly?<i> </i></li>
</ul>

<h3>Who we want to hear from</h3>

<p>We are&#160;seeking the views of those who have experience, expertise or interest in statutory declarations and the operation of the Act and the Regulations. This may include:</p>

<ul>
	<li>any member of the public who uses statutory declarations for business or personal purposes</li>
	<li>legal practitioners and peak legal bodies</li>
	<li>community associations and other interest groups</li>
	<li>academics</li>
	<li>government agencies.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to share your views</h3>

<p>We encourage you to provide your responses using the &#8216;Have your say&#8217; button on this page. This will take you to the consultation platform. Submitting your response through the platform helps us publish responses in a way that all users can access.</p>

<p>You do not need to answer every question. You may respond only to the questions that are relevant to you or your organisation.</p>

<p>We encourage you to refer to the discussion paper while you answer questions on the consultation platform. The discussion paper contains further background information about the consultation questions. You can download it above.</p>

<p>If you are unable to use the consultation platform, you may provide a written submission responding to the questions in the discussion paper by email to our Modernising Document Execution Section at <a href="mailto:statdec@ag.gov.au">statdec@ag.gov.au</a>.</p>
