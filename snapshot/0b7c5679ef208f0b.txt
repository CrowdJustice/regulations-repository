      <h2>Commissioner’s message</h2>

<p>One of the things I often reflect on in my role is how much the integrity of our legal system depends on the everyday decisions lawyers make – and on the support they receive to make those decisions well.&nbsp;</p>

<p>I’m&nbsp;mindful that many of you are currently applying to renew your&nbsp;practising&nbsp;certificates. Although this process can feel routine, it does play&nbsp;an important role&nbsp;in protecting the integrity of the profession.&nbsp;It’s&nbsp;a reminder of the trust your clients place in you to be honest,&nbsp;ethical&nbsp;and accountable, and of your role in upholding the rule of law.&nbsp;</p>

<p>In our&nbsp;<a href="https://lsbc.vic.gov.au/lawyers/risk-outlook/risk-outlook-2026" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Risk Outlook 2026 | VLSB+C">Risk Outlook 2026</a>, we&nbsp;highlight inadequate supervision as a significant risk, particularly for early career lawyers. Good supervision is not&nbsp;transactional;&nbsp;it&nbsp;isn’t&nbsp;just about&nbsp;ensuring&nbsp;tasks are done on time and to the correct&nbsp;standard.&nbsp;At its core,&nbsp;good supervision also encompasses&nbsp;care and responsibility – taking the time to guide, support and share knowledge.&nbsp;</p>

<p>When supervision is clear and consistent, early career lawyers are more confident,&nbsp;and it helps&nbsp;improve&nbsp;ethical&nbsp;decision-making,&nbsp;supporting&nbsp;lawyers to become more knowledgeable&nbsp;and better equipped to serve their clients. When it&nbsp;isn’t, the impacts can be serious&nbsp;and&nbsp;adversely effect everyone involved&nbsp;– for lawyers’ wellbeing, for clients and for trust in the profession.&nbsp;That’s&nbsp;why I encourage principals, supervisors, and early career lawyers to engage with our practical tools, which you can access below.&nbsp;</p>

<p>We also&nbsp;recognise&nbsp;in&nbsp;the&nbsp;Risk Outlook&nbsp;2026&nbsp;the complex environment that in-house&nbsp;lawyers&nbsp;(corporate lawyers)&nbsp;operate&nbsp;in&nbsp;and&nbsp;that a key pressure for&nbsp;corporate&nbsp;lawyers is managing employer expectations in a way that is consistent with their professional and ethical obligations.&nbsp;While these roles are rewarding, they also&nbsp;come&nbsp;with unique ethical challenges. I encourage you to access our newly designed resources for supporting ethical and effective in-house practice for corporate lawyers, their employers, and lawyers considering a move in-house. You can access these&nbsp;below.</p>

<p>Following&nbsp;this year’s Lawyer Census,&nbsp;I&nbsp;wish to thank&nbsp;everyone who took the time to respond.&nbsp;Your responses&nbsp;provide&nbsp;valuable insights into the issues shaping legal practice in Victoria – from client needs and technology, through to wellbeing, workplace&nbsp;conditions&nbsp;and professional boundaries. When lawyers share their experiences, we can more effectively target policies and resources to support the profession in a rapidly changing environment, track progress over time, and&nbsp;ultimately improve&nbsp;the experience of both lawyers and their clients.&nbsp;</p>

<p>I am pleased to acknowledge the&nbsp;<a href="https://lsbc.vic.gov.au/news-updates/news/sam-hay-kc-appointed-justice-supreme-court-victoria" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Sam Hay KC appointed as Justice of the Supreme Court of Victoria | VLSB+C">appointment of our former Board Chair&nbsp;Sam Hay KC</a>&nbsp;as a Justice of the Supreme Court of Victoria. Justice Hay’s appointment in late-April reflects his outstanding career at the Bar, and his deep commitment to the administration of justice.&nbsp;We warmly congratulate Sam on his appointment and acknowledge his contribution to our&nbsp;Board.&nbsp;</p>

<p>Finally, I’m excited&nbsp;to share with you our Innovate Reconciliation Action Plan. This is an important milestone for us as we&nbsp;continue to&nbsp;build awareness and&nbsp;First Peoples cultural capability inside and outside our organisation.&nbsp;During Reconciliation&nbsp;Week (27 May to 3 June) we wholeheartedly&nbsp;commit to being ‘All In’ and&nbsp;proudly&nbsp;reiterate our&nbsp;commitment&nbsp;to reconciliation, improving systems,&nbsp;and access to justice&nbsp;with and for First Peoples.&nbsp;</p>

<p><b>Fiona McLeay&nbsp;<br>
Board&nbsp;CEO and Commissioner&nbsp;</b></p>

									                                <div class="paragraph-description">
            <h2>Why kindness matters for early career lawyers and their supervisors</h2>

<p>We&nbsp;invite you to ‘Why kindness matters for early career lawyers and their supervisors’,&nbsp;a CPD&nbsp;webinar&nbsp;with Dr Nicki Macklin,&nbsp;researcher, advisor, and speaker focused on the human side of organisational performance.</p>

<p><a href="https://lsbc.vic.gov.au/research-centre/news/tired-unhealthy-workplaces-uncivil-behaviours-legal-profession" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="‘Tired of unhealthy workplaces’: uncivil behaviours in the legal profession | VLSB+C">Recent findings</a>&nbsp;from the Legal Services Research Centre show that while uncivil behaviour is common across the Victorian legal profession, young and emerging lawyers experience higher rates of workplace incivility.&nbsp;</p>

<p>As part of our ongoing support for early career lawyers and their supervisors,&nbsp;we’re&nbsp;offering this free, CPD-approved&nbsp;webinar, where&nbsp;Nicki will share her research on kindness as a form of social capital and a lever for performance and quality improvement.&nbsp;She’ll&nbsp;also provide practical tips for weaving kindness into the fabric of the supervisor/supervisee relationship, in ways that improve wellbeing and legal practise.</p>

<p>Kerri-anne&nbsp;Millard, our Executive Director of Policy and Outreach, will join&nbsp;Nicki to share&nbsp;<a href="https://lsbc.vic.gov.au/resources/early-career-lawyer-capability-framework" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Early Career Lawyer Capability Framework | VLSB+C">evidence-based guidance</a>&nbsp;developed by us to support early career lawyers and their supervisors to get the most out of supervised legal practise.</p>

<p>We invite you to share this invitation with the early career lawyers and supervisors in your organisation and professional networks.</p>

<p>When:&nbsp;Tuesday 9 June 2026,&nbsp;12.30pm&nbsp;– 1.30pm<br />
Where: online, link will be provided ahead of event&nbsp;</p>

            <h2>$1 million&nbsp;scam: a reminder to always verify payment details</h2>

<p>We continue to see Victorian law practices fall victim to cybersecurity breaches, resulting in significant losses of client trust money.</p>

<p>In one recent case, a law practice reported that almost $1 million was transferred from its trust account to a scammer’s bank account, instead of the intended recipient. Despite&nbsp;having cybersecurity measures in place, the law practice’s email system was compromised after a staff member was on the receiving end of a phishing&nbsp;scam.</p>

<p>The scammer gained access to the staff member’s email account and waited. When the staff member later emailed a payment requisition to the accounts team, the scammer intercepted the message and altered the bank details in the PDF. The scammer also contacted the intended recipient to say there might be a delay in payment because of a banking issue. This allowed time for the funds to be transferred overseas and become untraceable.</p>

<p>The fraud was only discovered weeks later when the rightful recipient contacted the law practice to ask why they&nbsp;hadn’t&nbsp;received the funds.</p>

<p>This sophisticated&nbsp;scam&nbsp;may have been prevented if the accounts team had strictly followed&nbsp;our minimum cybersecurity expectations, particularly by confirming bank details using verified contact information before processing the payment.</p>

<p><a class="newbtn readmore-btn" href="https://lsbc.vic.gov.au/lawyers/practising-law/cybersecurity" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Cybersecurity | VLSB+C">Review our cybersecurity information</a></p>

<h3>Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing obligations</h3>

<p>Law practices and lawyers providing&nbsp;<a href="https://www.austrac.gov.au/glossary/designated-service" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Designated service | AUSTRAC">designated services</a>&nbsp;should be aware that new anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing obligations&nbsp;commence&nbsp;on 1 July 2026. If you provide designated services, you must&nbsp;<a href="https://www.austrac.gov.au/new-austrac/enrol-or-register/enrol-us" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Enrol with us overview | AUSTRAC">enrol</a>&nbsp;with AUSTRAC and&nbsp;comply with&nbsp;the new AML/CTF regime from that date.</p>

<p>By 1 July 2026, affected law practices and lawyers need to have the following arrangements in place:&nbsp;</p>

<ul>
	<li>be enrolled with AUSTRAC and have an AML/CTF program in place, whether based on AUSTRAC's program starter kit or your own program&nbsp;</li>
	<li>have an AML/CTF compliance officer&nbsp;</li>
	<li>train staff on the program, internal&nbsp;processes&nbsp;and relevant AML/CTF risks&nbsp;</li>
	<li>be&nbsp;ready to undertake customer due diligence and report suspicious matters.</li>
</ul>

<p>If you are unsure whether the new laws apply to your practice, you can use AUSTRAC's&nbsp;<a href="https://www.austrac.gov.au/check-if-you-need-enrol-and-register" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Check if you need to enrol and register | AUSTRAC">online checker</a>&nbsp;and review its&nbsp;<a href="https://www.austrac.gov.au/new-austrac/designated-services-newly-regulated-entities" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Designated services for newly regulated entities | AUSTRAC">guidance</a>&nbsp;on designated services.&nbsp;</p>

<h3>New laws for Non-Disclosure Agreements&nbsp;</h3>

<p>The&nbsp;<a href="https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/as-made/acts/restricting-non-disclosure-agreements-sexual-harassment-work-act-2025" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" title="Restricting Non-disclosure Agreements (Sexual Harassment at Work) Act 2025  | legislation.vic.gov.au"><em>Restricting Non‑disclosure Agreements (Sexual Harassment at Work) Act 2025</em>&nbsp;(Vic)</a>&nbsp;commences&nbsp;on&nbsp;1 July 2026.
