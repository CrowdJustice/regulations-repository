    <h2>Overview</h2>
    <p>The Australian Government is currently preparing Australia&#8217;s Seventh Periodic Report under the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR). Australia&#8217;s report will detail actions Australian governments have taken in response to the List of Issues Prior to Reporting (LOIPR) provided by the United Nations Human Rights Committee (the Committee). The report will cover the period November&#160;2017 to August&#160;2026. It is due to the Committee by 7&#160;August&#160;2026.</p>

<p>In line with article 40 of the ICCPR, Australia must submit periodic reports detailing the legal, judicial and policy measures we have taken to carry out our obligations under the ICCPR. After submitting the report, Australia will appear before the Committee to have constructive discussions. The Committee will evaluate Australia&#8217;s compliance with the ICCPR and provide concluding observations and recommendations.</p>

<p>The process aims to evaluate how Australia implements its obligations under the ICCPR. It provides an opportunity for constructive dialogue on how well Australia complies with the ICCPR and how we can improve.</p>

<p>The LOIPR is a document that the Committee sends before the periodic report is due, which guides the content of the report and allows the State to focus on key areas by highlighting priority areas for consideration. The Committee establishes the LOIPR based on documentation from Non-Government Organisations, UN agencies and National Human Rights Institutions. During its 144th session (23&#160;June to 17&#160;July&#160;2025), the Committee adopted <a href="user_uploads/iccpr-australias-seventh-report-loipr.pdf" target="_blank">Australia&#8217;s LOIPR for its Seventh Periodic Report [PDF 202KB]</a>.</p>

<ul>
	<li><a href="user_uploads/iccpr-public-consultation-fact-sheet-may-2026.pdf" target="_blank">Fact sheet: List of Issues Prior to Reporting [PDF 214KB]</a></li>
	<li><a href="user_uploads/iccpr-public-consultation-fact-sheet-may-2026.docx" target="_blank">Fact sheet: List of Issues Prior to Reporting [DOCX 104KB]</a></li>
</ul>

<div class="cs-factbank">
<h4 class="cs-factbank-header dss-heading-x-small"><a class="collapsed hide-when-no-js" data-toggle="collapse" href="#fact-bank-1">Fact sheet: List of Issues Prior to Reporting </a><span class="show-when-no-js">More Information</span></h4>

<div class="cs-factbank-content collapse" id="fact-bank-1">
<div class="cs-factbank-content-inner">
<p>This fact sheet summarises the key requests in the List of Issues Prior to Reporting (LOIPR). It explains what information the UN Human Rights Committee (HRC) has asked Australia to include in its next International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) report.</p>

<h2>What is the List of Issues Prior to Reporting?</h2>

<p>The LOIPR is a set of requests for information from the HRC. The LOIPR asks Australia to provide specific information on how it is implementing the ICCPR.</p>

<p>Australia must respond to these requests in its next report under the ICCPR.</p>

<h3>Part A - General human rights framework and implementation</h3>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 1</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on:</p>

<ul>
	<li>any major legal or institutional changes affecting human rights since the last review</li>
	<li>how ICCPR rights are applied by laws and courts</li>
	<li>what steps have been taken to implement past UN recommendations and decisions to ensure effective remedies for individuals.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Part B - Specific information on the implementation of articles 1 to 27 of the Covenant</h3>

<h4>A. Constitutional and legal frameworks</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 2</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how it ensures that federal and state laws are compatible with the ICCPR. This includes how new laws are assessed for human rights compliance and how awareness of the ICCPR is promoted among courts and law enforcement.</p>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 3</h5>

<p>The HRC seeks information on whether Australia intends to withdraw its remaining reservations to certain ICCPR rights and how it regularly reviews whether those reservations remain necessary and justified.</p>

<h4>B. National human rights institutions (art. 2)</h4>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on what steps it has taken to strengthen the independence of the Australian Human Rights Commission and ensure it has sufficient funding, staff, and technical resources to carry out its human rights mandate in line with the Paris Principles.</p>

<p>The HRC also seeks an update on Australia&#8217;s response to recommendations to expand the Commission&#8217;s mandate, so it covers all relevant international human rights treaties.</p>

<h4>C. Non-discrimination (arts. 2, 19, 20 and 26)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 5 &#8211; Non&#8209;discrimination protections in law</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on:</p>

<ul>
	<li>how federal law provides comprehensive protection against all forms of discrimination under the ICCPR</li>
	<li>how people are supported to report discrimination</li>
	<li>what data shows about complaints, investigations, penalties, and remedies for victims.</li>
</ul>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 6 &#8211; Racial discrimination, hate speech, and profiling</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how it addresses racial discrimination, hate speech, and incitement to violence. This includes:</p>

<ul>
	<li>law enforcement action</li>
	<li>preventing racial profiling</li>
	<li>training officials</li>
	<li>promoting tolerance and diversity through public education.</li>
</ul>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 7 &#8211; Sexual orientation and gender identity</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how it:</p>

<ul>
	<li>prevents discrimination and stigma based on sexual orientation or gender identity, including in healthcare and education</li>
	<li>ensures timely access to gender&#8209;affirming healthcare without unnecessary legal barriers.</li>
</ul>

<h4>A. Violence against women, including domestic violence (Arts. 3,6,7 and 26)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 8 &#8211; Gender&#8209;based violence</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how it is preventing and responding to violence against women. This includes:</p>

<ul>
	<li>implementation of the National Plan to End Violence Against Women and children (2022-2032)</li>
	<li>data collection</li>
	<li>victim support services</li>
	<li>accountability for perpetrators.</li>
</ul>

<h4>D. Counter-Terrorism measures (arts. 2, 9, 12, 14, 17 and 22)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 9</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how its Counter-Terrorism laws and expanded surveillance powers comply with human rights principles contained in the ICCPR such as necessity, proportionality, non&#8209;discrimination, due process, and independent oversight.</p>

<h4>E. Right to life (art. 6)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 10 &#8211; Climate change, environment, and the right to life</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to explain provide information on how it:</p>

<ul>
	<li>protects the right to life in the context of climate change and environmental harm, particularly for vulnerable communities</li>
	<li>avoids returning people to countries where environmental risks may cause serious harm.</li>
</ul>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 11 &#8211; Use of force and deaths in custody</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how allegations of excessive use of force and deaths in custody are independently investigated. This includes outcomes for accountability, compensation and systemic reform.</p>

<h4>F. Prohibition of cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment (arts. 2,3,7,9,17, 24 and 26)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 12 &#8211; Sterilisation and intersex medical treatment</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how it prevents non&#8209;therapeutic sterilisation of people with disabilities. It also asks for information on how Australia regulates irreversible medical procedures on intersex children in line with human rights standards.</p>

<h4>G. Treatment of aliens, including refugees and asylum-seekers (arts. 2,7,9,10 and 17)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 13 &#8211; Refugee protection and offshore processing</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how it ensures fair refugee processes and respects the principle of non&#8209;refoulement. This includes maritime interceptions, offshore processing arrangements and transfers to third countries.</p>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 14 &#8211; Immigration detention</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how its immigration detention laws comply with the ICCPR. This includes:</p>

<ul>
	<li>limiting detention to what is necessary</li>
	<li>expanding alternatives to detention</li>
	<li>ensuring children are not detained</li>
	<li>providing effective judicial review.</li>
</ul>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 15 &#8211; Conditions of immigration detention</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide data on immigration detention and explain how it ensures humane detention conditions, access to healthcare and accountability for the use of force. This includes remedies for people affected by abuse or ill&#8209;treatment.</p>

<h4>H. Liberty and security of person and treatment of persons deprived of their liberty (art. 7, 9, 10, 14,26 and 27)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 16 &#8211; Detention and prison conditions</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to explain how it:</p>

<ul>
	<li>limits the use and length of pre&#8209;trial detention</li>
	<li>improves prison conditions</li>
	<li>reduces overcrowding and solitary confinement</li>
	<li>ensures adequate mental health care and support for people in custody.</li>
</ul>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 17 &#8211; People with disabilities in detention</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on the safeguards in place for people with disabilities who are detained. This includes protections against indefinite detention, involuntary treatment and forced interventions, as well as the availability of community&#8209;based alternatives.</p>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 18 &#8211; Indigenous over&#8209;incarceration</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on what measures it is taking to address the over&#8209;representation of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples in prisons. This includes sentencing practices, use of community&#8209;based alternatives and access to culturally appropriate legal services.</p>

<h4>I. Right to privacy (art. 17)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 19 &#8211; Right to privacy</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how its laws and practices protect individuals from arbitrary or unlawful interference with privacy. This includes new privacy remedies, online safety regulation and safeguards for surveillance, data access and digital harms.</p>

<h4>Freedom of expression (art. 19)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 20</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how its laws and practices, including online safety measures and social media age restrictions, comply with the ICCPR by protecting freedom of expression, access to information and privacy while appropriately safeguarding children.</p>

<h4>J. Freedom of assembly (art. 21)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 21</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on how anti&#8209;protest laws, including recent New South Wales legislation, comply with the ICCPR right to peaceful assembly and do not disproportionately restrict the rights to protest, freedom of expression or freedom of association.</p>

<h4>K. Rights of the child (arts. 9, 14 and 24)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 22</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on what steps it has taken to:</p>

<ul>
	<li>raise the minimum age of criminal responsibility</li>
	<li>ensure that children are detained only as a last resort</li>
	<li>prevent solitary confinement of children</li>
	<li>hold children separately from adults.</li>
</ul>

<h4>L. Right to participate in public life (art. 25)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 23</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on:</p>

<ul>
	<li>how many people are excluded from voting because of disability</li>
	<li>what measures are in place to ensure that any restrictions are reasonable, proportionate and directly related to a person&#8217;s capacity to vote.</li>
</ul>

<h4>M. Rights of minorities and Indigenous Peoples (arts. 2, 9, 14, 26 and 27)</h4>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 24 &#8211; Indigenous participation after the referendum</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on what alternative measures are being taken to ensure effective participation of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples in decision-making following the 2023 referendum.</p>

<h5 style="font-size:1.3rem;font-weight: bold;">Paragraph 25 &#8211; Native title and reparations</h5>

<p>The HRC asks Australia to provide information on:</p>

<ul>
	<li>how native title law complies with human rights obligations</li>
	<li>what steps are being taken to establish reparations, including compensation for members of the Stolen Generations.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Why we are consulting</h3>

<p>The ICCPR reporting process is an opportunity to engage with the Committee on the international stage to highlight Australia&#8217;s ongoing compliance with civil and political rights domestically and internationally, while acknowledging areas for improvement.</p>

<p>The consultation process will help ensure that the Australian Government can effectively consider the views provided by the public on Australia&#8217;s draft Seventh Periodic Report.</p>

<p>We are seeking your views and feedback on the following draft ICCPR report.</p>

<ul>
	<li><a href="user_uploads/consultation-draft-australias-seventh-periodic-report-under-the-iccpr.pdf" target="_blank">Australia&#8217;s draft Seventh Periodic Report under the ICCPR (Draft) [PDF 716KB]</a></li>
	<li><a href="user_uploads/consultation-draft-australias-seventh-periodic-report-under-the-iccpr.docx" target="_blank">Australia&#8217;s draft Seventh Periodic Report under the ICCPR (Draft) [DOCX 130KB]</a></li>
</ul>

<p>We want to hear from anyone with an interest in Australia&#8217;s human rights issues. This includes:</p>

<ul>
	<li>individuals with lived experience</li>
	<li>human rights experts</li>
	<li>peak bodies</li>
	<li>advocates</li>
	<li>the legal sector</li>
	<li>advocacy providers</li>
	<li>academics</li>
	<li>non-government organisations</li>
	<li>government organisations.</li>
</ul>

<p>Your feedback and perspectives are invaluable to the ICCPR process.</p>

<h3>How to share your views</h3>

<p>We invite you to share your views using the &#8216;Have your say&#8217; link below. You can use the free text fields to share your views and provide feedback, or you can upload a single submission.</p>

<h3>Publishing responses</h3>

<p>You can submit feedback to be shared anonymously. We intend to publish all responses at the end of the consultation period on our consultation hub. There will be some mandatory questions to confirm whether you agree to your response being made public.</p>

<p>If you would like part of your response to remain in confidence, please provide that information separately and clearly indicate which information you would like to remain confidential. Please do not include personal information about other individuals in your feedback or in the body of your submission.</p>

<h3>Next steps</h3>

<p>We will consider all feedback received as we finalise the draft ICCPR Seventh Periodic Report.</p>
